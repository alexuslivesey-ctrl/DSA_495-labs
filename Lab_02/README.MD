## 1. Topic modeling

### Q1. The collection and its representations

Report the number of FAQ documents and how many were shortened to create
`topic_text`. Explain why topic modeling uses opening excerpts while RAG
chunks the complete FAQ text. What information might the opening excerpts
miss when you interpret topics?

**Response:**

[300 FAQ documents exist in the data. 194 documents were shortened to create
`topic text`. Topic modeling using MINILM can only input a maximum length of 256 
tokens. This means FAQ documents inserted into the MINILM topic model may cut off
important information. Using open excerpt ensures that all the important information, 
such as the purpose of the document or key terms, is at the top of the document and 
thus is not omitted from the analysis. RAG uses more specific details and can search 
deep in documents to find specific information needed to make evaluations.]

### Q7. Retrieval depth, recommendation, and limitation

Begin with one sentence explaining what `k` controls. Using the side-by-side
answers in the notebook, describe one change—or lack of change—between `k=3`
and `k=5`. Which retrieval depth would you choose, or is the evidence insufficient
to prefer one? Support your recommendation with a specific answer or passage.
State what stayed fixed and identify **one limitation** of this two-question comparison.

**Response:**

K controls how many chunks the model takes. A discrepancy between k=3 and k=5 
is that they use different citations to cite the second claim in the passages. 
They both say " vary depending on the type of loan product," but k=3 cites 
[C0176] whereas k=5 [C0179].

I would choose a higher k value, in this case k=5, because it can grasp more 
evidence than k=3 can. An example to support this is the side-by-side comparison
for E2. The RAG model that used k=3 did not specify the detailed factors that affect
credit score ranges. Whereas in the k=5 model, it claimed that "factors impacting 
credit scores, such as payment history and debt levels." What is more, k=5 also
provides a list of examples of methods to obtain credit scores, but k-3 does not. 

A limitation of this two-question comparison is that it is not tested on more 
documents in the corpus with potentially different information and context. It
